# How To Read This

*A first-reader's note. Not canon, not graded, not in any lane. Written from an adversarial
read that reached the wrong verdict several times before reaching the documents; every
correction below is already stated somewhere in the corpus, and the citation is given. Its only
claim is to save the next reader the same sequence.*

---

## What it is

Archaeology, not argument.

The thesis is about how theories work: **an effective compression fuses, and effectiveness is
what hides the fusion.** GR's equations close while ordering, influence, flow, rendering, uptake
and constitution stay bundled — *"success itself removed the pressure to separate them"* (A0 §1).
So the most successful theory is the one most likely carrying fusions nobody noticed, and they
are invisible precisely because nothing ever went wrong.

You cannot target a buried site. You dig where compression was most effective — which is why the
targets are the spectacular results: Hawking, Unruh, tunneling, Maxwell's demon, GW170817. Most
trenches come up empty. The empty ones are reported, which is what makes this a survey rather
than a case.

The photon seed is not a claim. It is the existence proof that the question has at least one
answer: here is a fusion GR carries — cause and flow — that nothing ever forced apart.

## Six ways to misread it

**1. Grading A0 and A1 by empirical yield.** Both disclaim confirmatory weight in writing. A1 §2:
*"the photon seed is a launch-site carve, not a witness for itself… seed gives the defusion;
downstream landings carry any weight."* A0 §13 lists what A0 does not do, and *claim experimental
confirmation* is on it. They are method documents. Measuring them by results is a category error.

**2. Reading C-notes as claims.** c1's second line reads *Grade: probe / exploratory export*, and
A0 §11 defines the term literally: *"probe: bounds an interior term without reaching the
boundary; carries bounding weight only."* c3 delivers exactly that — `ε ≤ Δt/T`, one edge, no
lower bound. A test pit, labeled, yielding a bound. Not the corpus's central claim.

**3. Reading "owned" as a priority claim.** A0 §10 says *framework-owned **if earned*** and lists
route, composition, forced dependency, missing/extra — derivation provenance, not novelty. The
README forecloses the other reading: *"success = prior role-carve + clean missing/extra + stated
could-have-failed — **not novelty alone**."* Reaching a published theorem by a different route is
corroboration, and CLAIMS has a grade for it: `lineage-witness`.

**4. Using "relabel" in the ordinary sense.** Here it means a renaming that **changes no
commitment** — *"if nothing must follow, the reading changed no commitment anywhere"* (A0). A
re-description that resolves a live confusion changes commitments and is the opposite of relabel.
The confusions being resolved are not slogan-level: the Hartman dispute ran thirty years among
professionals and produced published superluminal-signalling claims; QEC milestone ambiguity is
live in a funded field right now.

**5. Reading the empty notes as failures.** b7 §8 runs the instrument at Unruh — the most tempting
target available — and reports *no missing/extra remainder*. b5's torsion lane was excavated with
eleven readout notes and thirteen CSVs, then archived wholesale as non-witness-bearing. Those are
the survey's negative results, and they are what make it an instrument rather than a hammer: it
can tell fused-and-hiding-something from fused-and-fine, and it says which.

**6. Reading breadth as lack of focus, or prose as overclaim.** The breadth is survey. And a note
runs cold at its header, wide in its body, cold again at its ledger block — that is one narrowing,
not three registers disagreeing. TONE.md specifies it: *"do not front-load caveats; keep
boundaries, but place them after the core claim has landed."*

## The terms are literal

Not decorative, and not metaphor. USES states the rule for closure failures and it generalises:
*"without the literal-cycle rule this is metaphor; with it, it is a taxonomy of which role fails
to close."*

There is a reason stronger than tidiness. **Metaphor is compression** — it fuses a source and a
target and hides the seam, which is the exact pathology under audit. A corpus about what
compression conceals cannot run on metaphors without becoming the thing it is auditing.

So read every term at face value, and read the README glossary's **"Not meant"** column as
load-bearing. `flow` is proper-time accrual and nothing else. `information` is not any
correlation, record, or Shannon bit. `failed floor` means a surface you dug for and did not reach.

---

## What the accounting is for

You cannot control what is in the ground. You can control whether you know what you brought and
what you found. That is the whole function of the layer blocks (owned / projected / imported =
stratigraphy), the grades (provenance), and CLAIMS' rule that lineage-witness rows are
**non-additive** — contacts do not stack into confidence.

It is also why the method is retrodictive by nature rather than by concession. You reconstruct a
site from what was left behind; you cannot predict your way to a buried one. *Chronology is not
evidence; lineage is the criterion* is the same sentence as: what matters is not when a thing was
written but what it was found under.

## What survives as criticism

Three things, after everything above is granted:

1. **One trench went deep on unsecured stratigraphy.** The C and D lanes run ten notes down
   through c1's Cost 0 — the fabric/content split, *chosen, coined in that note, unaudited
   upstream*. Digging fast past an unread layer is a real hazard, and the corpus books it in the
   open as a cost rather than absorbing it.
2. **Four corroborations left unbanked.** Greenberg (PRD 73, 087701) reaches the microcausality
   reduction from covariance of time-ordered products; the Segré classification of `g⁻¹f` is
   standard in massive gravity; Sommerfeld–Brillouin and Winful (Phys. Rep. 436) own the
   tunneling resolution; the generalized Landauer bound owns imperfect erasure. None demotes a
   layer. All four are independent arrival at the same place, which is support this corpus wants
   and currently forfeits. The gap is specifically in formal and axiomatic literature, not in
   phenomenology, which is cited scrupulously.
3. **Two stale lines.** b9 §6 restates a claim b9 §2 already narrowed away; b5's header still
   reads *structural-bet candidate* against D9's *archived sensitivity*.

Everything else this reader raised dissolved on contact with the documents.

## The one thing to hold onto

The question is not whether any given unfusing is right or new. It is:

> **What did GR fuse that we did not notice?**

The notes are attempts to answer it. The ledger is the record of which attempts found something
and which found nothing. Both are results.
